# Finding a job in Poland as an EU citizen

## Your right to work

As an EU national you may take up employment or work self-employed in Poland
**without a work permit**, and you must be treated the same as Polish nationals
in access to jobs, pay and working conditions.

## Staying while you look for work

- You can stay in Poland for up to 3 months without registering.
- If you are looking for work, authorities normally allow a jobseeker period of
  around 6 months; beyond that you may be asked to show that you are actively
  job-hunting and have a genuine chance of being hired.
- Jobseekers cannot be expelled while they can show a genuine chance of finding
  work.

## Where to search

- **EURES**, the EU job mobility portal, lists vacancies across 31 European
  countries, free for jobseekers, and offers advisers who help with CVs,
  applications and relocation questions.
- **Public employment services**: the local district labour office (powiatowy
  urząd pracy) registers jobseekers and gives EU citizens the same assistance as
  Polish nationals. Registering there is required if you are transferring
  unemployment benefit from your home country.
- **Commercial job boards** are where most private-sector hiring happens in
  Poland. Widely used ones include Pracuj.pl (general), and for IT roles
  JustJoin.it, No Fluff Jobs and LinkedIn.

## Transferring unemployment benefit

If you receive unemployment benefit from another EU country, you can usually
continue to receive it while job-hunting in Poland for at least 3 months (often
extendable up to 6), provided you register with the employment service here.

## Practical notes

- In IT, shared-service and international roles, English is often the working
  language; other sectors usually expect Polish.
- Employment contracts (umowa o pracę) include employer-paid social insurance;
  B2B contracts pay more gross but you handle your own ZUS and tax.
- You will need a PESEL number for payroll and health-insurance registration.
