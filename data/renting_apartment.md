# Renting an apartment in Poland

Renting (najem) is the usual first step after arriving. A tenancy agreement also
often serves as proof of address for a PESEL application or address registration.

## Finding a place

- Common listing portals include OLX, Otodom and Gumtree; many flats are also let
  through agencies (which may charge a commission of about one month's rent).
- Rents are highest in Warsaw, Kraków, Wrocław, the Tri-City (Gdańsk/Gdynia/Sopot)
  and Poznań.

## The tenancy agreement (umowa najmu)

- Get a written agreement stating the rent, the deposit (kaucja, typically one to
  two months), the notice period, and what the rent includes.
- "Czynsz" can be ambiguous: it may mean the rent to the landlord and/or the
  building administration fee (media/administracja). Clarify which bills — heating,
  water, gas, electricity, internet — are included and which you pay separately.
- A registered tenancy agreement can be used as a legal basis for a PESEL and for
  address registration (zameldowanie).

## Deposit and handover

- Pay the deposit and document the flat's condition at handover (photos, meter
  readings) so the deposit is returned fairly at the end.

## Notes

- "Najem okazjonalny" is a special registered short-form lease that gives the
  landlord stronger eviction protection; it requires a notarial statement from the
  tenant.

Source: summarised from general Polish rental-market practice and gov.pl consumer
guidance. Not legal advice.
