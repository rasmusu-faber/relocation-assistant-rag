# Tax number (NIP) and income tax (PIT) in Poland

Poland has two tax-relevant identifiers for individuals: the PESEL (used for most
people) and the NIP (Numer Identyfikacji Podatkowej).

## PESEL vs NIP

- Most employees settle their income tax using their **PESEL** number.
- A **NIP** is required if you run a business, are self-employed, or otherwise act
  as a registered taxpayer or VAT payer. Employers and companies always use a NIP.

## Getting a NIP

- Individuals not running a business usually do not need to apply separately — a
  NIP is assigned when required (e.g. when registering a sole proprietorship via
  the CEIDG business register).
- Businesses receive a NIP through the tax office (Urząd Skarbowy) or automatically
  on company registration.

## Income tax (PIT)

- Employment income is taxed under the progressive PIT scale (two brackets), with
  the employer withholding advances each month.
- The annual PIT return is filed after the tax year; the tax office pre-fills much
  of it via the online Twój e-PIT service.
- Tax residency generally applies if you live in Poland more than 183 days per year
  or have your centre of vital interests there.

## Notes

- A PESEL is enough for a standard employee; you do not need a NIP just to be
  employed on an umowa o pracę.
